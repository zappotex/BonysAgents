# Exercise: browsing more privately

A VPN is an important building block – but only together with a few habits does your browsing really become more
private. Try out at least **three** of the points. Your tunnel stays connected meanwhile.

## 1. DNS through the tunnel

Before a page loads, the computer asks which address belongs to its name (**DNS**). If these requests bypass the
VPN, your internet provider still sees where you go.

Open `https://www.dnsleaktest.com` and click **"Standard test"**. Good: **only your VPN provider's servers** appear,
not your internet provider's.

## 2. Trackers

Open `https://coveryourtracks.eff.org` and click **"TEST YOUR BROWSER"** (takes about half a minute).

- The Brave browser blocks ad trackers and invisible trackers – the shield icon on the right of the address bar shows
  how many.
- But the test also shows: your browser often has a **unique fingerprint**. A VPN doesn't help with that.

## 3. Keeping sign-ins and cookies apart

Anyone who **signs in** somewhere is recognized – no matter via which address. So keep things apart:

- **Private window with Tor** in Brave: menu (☰) → **"New private window with Tor"** (Alt + Shift + N). No history,
  no cookies afterwards – and the way also goes through the Tor network (slower).
- For private things where you sign in, a **separate Agent PC** – separate from the one you try new things in.

## 4. Unknown things in a throwaway PC

Dubious sites or downloads? In the main window, create an Agent PC from a **template**, look at everything there – and
**delete** it afterwards. Nothing is left behind.

## 5. Even more private: the Tor Browser

Bony's Agents offers the **Tor Browser** as an app (only on computers with an Intel/AMD processor): in the main window
next to "Programs" → **"Add programs …"** → "Tor Browser".

::: tipp
**Private doesn't mean anonymous.** The VPN provider and your browser's fingerprint remain – knowing that honestly is
already a big step.
:::
